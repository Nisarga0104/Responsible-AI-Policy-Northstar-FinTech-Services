# Policy Structure

The original final policy is preserved in `07_Final/Responsible_AI_Policy_Final.pdf`.

This Markdown representation maps its operational structure:

1. **Purpose** — establish consistent AI risk management across development, procurement, deployment and monitoring.
2. **Scope** — internally developed and third-party AI used in Northstar operations.
3. **Policy Principles** — fairness; transparency; explainability; contestability; accountability; human oversight; privacy; security; safety; robustness; accessibility.
4. **Roles and Responsibilities** — named accountability across business, governance, technical, legal, security, risk, procurement, audit and employee functions.
5. **AI Risk Classification** — four internal governance tiers.
6. **AI Lifecycle Governance** — ideation through retirement.
7. **Data Governance** — quality, provenance, privacy, access, retention and sensitive-data controls.
8. **Model/System Governance** — validation, testing, monitoring, security, versioning and documentation.
9. **Human Oversight** — mandatory review for adverse actions, High-tier decisions and low-confidence cases.
10. **Incident Management** — identification, escalation, containment, investigation, remediation and lessons learned.
11. **Third-Party AI** — vendor due diligence plus Northstar's own risk and lifecycle controls.
12. **Employee Responsibilities** — approved-use boundaries, data handling, reporting and AI literacy.
13. **Exceptions** — written, time-bound, risk-justified approval.
14. **Enforcement** — policy violations handled through employee/vendor processes.
15. **Review Cycle** — at least annual review plus event-driven review.
16. **References** — framework, standards and regulatory sources.

## Key design distinction

The policy's internal risk classification is explicitly not treated as equivalent to an external legal risk taxonomy. It is an enterprise governance mechanism used to determine the level of scrutiny required.
